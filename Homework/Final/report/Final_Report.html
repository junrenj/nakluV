Final - SSAO & SSDO by Jimmy Jian (junrej)

In the final project, I integrated Screen Space Ambient Occlusion (SSAO) and Screen Space Directional Occlusion (SSDO) into the rendering engine. As both techniques rely on G-buffer data, the rendering pipeline was restructured to use a deferred rendering architecture.

My Final Scene Final-create

I recreated the Research Sector atrium from the video game Control. This scene features rich architectural facades, making it particularly suitable for testing SSAO and SSDO.

    GBuffer / Direct Lighting Buffer

    To support deferred rendering, I restructured the original forward rendering pipeline. The current rendering pipeline executes in the following order:
    First, the shadow map pass is rendered. Next, the G-buffer pass is generated.
    Then, using the G-buffer and shadow information, the direct lighting buffer is computed.
    After that, SSAO and SSDO are applied.
    Finally, the combined pipeline integrates all the intermediate results to produce the final rendered image.

    Visual Difference

    From the visual results shown below, the main idea of SSAO is to compute an occlusion factor, which effectively darkens the scene. In contrast, SSDO aims to determine whether there are nearby surfaces that can act as emissive light sources, thereby simulating indirect illumination.

    Performance Difference

    The chart shows that the frame time of both SSAO and SSDO increases with the sample count, indicating a positive correlation between sampling rate and performance cost. SSDO consistently incurs higher costs than SSAO and scales more steeply, reflecting its greater computational complexity.
