    Loading light objects A3-load

    Light data is loaded from the S72 file as S72::Light, then converted by URenderExtractor into engine-side objects (ULight). These are collected in URenderScene and transformed into FLightProxy for rendering. For lights that cast shadows, additional resources (FShadowResource) are created. Finally, the data is uploaded to the GPU as structured buffers and shadow maps.

    Adding Shadows for Spot Lights A3-shadows

    Shadow maps are rendered in the render function before the Lambert pipeline. I use a dedicated shadow pipeline with a shadow.vert shader to render depth information from the light's point of view.
    To reduce shadow artifacts, I apply both depth bias and normal bias to mitigate self-shadowing issues (shadow acne) on lit surfaces.
    Cube map textures are provided to materials through a descriptor set, allowing shaders to directly sample the data.
    For PCF filtering, I use a 2x2 sampling pattern with uniform weights to smooth shadow edges.

Attempt to separate the performance impact of shadow map rendering and shadow map sampling (e.g., by testing the same scenes with per-frame rendered shadow maps and pre-rendered shadow maps). Which is larger?
    Answer:
    Clearly, generating shadow maps is more expensive. The performance cost without shadows mainly comes from iterating over lights in the shader.
    Fixed shadows only incur a noticeable cost when the shadow maps are created; afterward, the performance increase is primarily due to sampling the shadow maps.
    For dynamic shadows, the shadow maps must be regenerated every frame. In addition, each light has its own shadow map, and generating each one requires traversing the scene and rendering depth for all objects. As a result, the overhead is significantly higher.
